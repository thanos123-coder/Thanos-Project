        Prompt 2 of 7421 / 650 words

        The lessons we take from obstacles we encounter can be fundamental to later success. Recount a time when you faced a challenge, setback, or failure. How did it affect you, and what did you learn from the experience?

        The water reached the fourth stair before anyone in my grandmother's building woke up. I was sixteen, visiting for the summer, and I remember the sound more than the sight: a low, patient gurgle, like the river was clearing its throat. By morning, their was a brown line across every door on the ground floor, and Mrs. Alves, a neighbor who's phone had been charging in the kitchen, had lost everything she couldn't carry upstairs.

        In today's rapidly evolving world, technology plays a pivotal role in helping communities become more resilient. But in my grandmother's town, the warning system was a man with a megaphone and a bicycle. The river authority published it's sensor readings online every fifteen minutes. Nobody read them at 3 a.m., due to the fact that nobody is awake at 3 a.m.

        Ever since I was a little kid I have loved taking things apart, but I had never built anything that mattered to someone else. That autumn I taught myself enough Python to pull the readings every five minutes and compare them with the levels from past floods. When the river rose faster than a threshold I had set, the system would send a text. The first real alert was sent by the system at 3:12 a.m. on a Tuesday in November, and it went to eleven people, most of them my grandmother's friends, three of whom called her to ask what on earth a "TideLine" was and whether they needed to pay for it.

        It also failed. In February the sensor upstream went offline and my code read the silence as calm water. Nobody was hurt, but alot of people got a text saying everything was fine while the car park flooded. I learned a lot about responsibility. I rewrote the logic so that missing data triggers a warning instead of a reassurance, and I started calling the river authority every time a sensor dropped, until a technician named Paulo started calling me first.

        This experience taught me that perseverance and determination are the keys to overcoming any obstacle. What it really taught me is narrower and more useful: a system is only as honest as its worst moment. TideLine now has 2,400 subscribers. I check it the way my grandmother checks the sky, and I want to study physics so the next thing I build understands the water as well as it understands the code. I realized that I wanted to use my skills to make a meaningful difference in the lives of others.
